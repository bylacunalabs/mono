How it works

Every word lands in the same place.

Your eyes lose time hopping across a page. Mono puts each word on one spot, with the red letter, where your gaze naturally lands, always in the centre.

Install

On your phone in under a minute.

No app store. Mono installs from your browser, gets its own icon and works offline.

  1. Open Mono in Safari

    Go to mono.lacunalabs.it.

  2. Tap Share

    The square with an arrow up , in the bottom bar (top right on iPad).

  3. Choose “Add to Home Screen”

    Scroll the list if you don't see it.

  4. Tap “Add”

    Mono is now on your Home Screen. Open it once online, then it works offline.

  1. Open Mono in Chrome

    Go to mono.lacunalabs.it.

  2. Tap the menu

    The three dots at the top right. If Mono shows an Install banner, tap that instead.

  3. Choose “Install app”

    Some versions say “Add to Home screen”.

  4. Tap “Install”

    Mono is now on your Home screen. Open it once online, then it works offline.

  1. Open Mono in Chrome or Edge

    Go to mono.lacunalabs.it.

  2. Click the install icon

    It's at the right end of the address bar. Then click Install.

  3. On a Mac with Safari

    Use File → Add to Dock instead (Safari 17 or later).

Your books stay on your device. Before clearing site data or removing the app, back them up to a file in Settings.

Research

Studied since 1970.

Showing words one at a time is called RSVP. These are the papers behind Mono.

RSVP was introduced: words shown one by one at a fixed spot.

Forster, K. I. (1970). Visual perception of rapidly presented word sequences of varying complexity. Perception & Psychophysics, 8(4), 215–221.

Reading without eye movements along a line is possible.

Rubin, G. S. & Turano, K. (1992). Reading without saccadic eye movements. Vision Research, 32(5), 895–902.

Words are recognised fastest near their middle, slightly left. That is the red letter.

O'Regan, J. K. & Jacobs, A. M. (1992). Optimal viewing position effect in word recognition: A challenge to current theory. Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance, 18(1), 185–197.

The honest limit. A major review finds that reading faster costs comprehension, and that not being able to look back hurts it more. So Mono starts at a calm 300 WPM, lets you tap to pause and see the whole text, and restarts a few words back after a pause.

Rayner, K., Schotter, E. R., Masson, M. E. J., Potter, M. C. & Treiman, R. (2016). So much to read, so little time: How do we read, and can speed reading help? Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 17(1), 4–34.
